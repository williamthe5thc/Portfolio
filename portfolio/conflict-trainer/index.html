Conflict Resolution Trainer: Branching Workplace Scenarios

A branching-scenario simulator for practicing workplace conflict. Learners choose how to respond, see how the choice plays out, and get feedback on the conflict strategy they used.

Opens the live simulator. All three scenarios are playable.

The problem

I want to help people handle conflict better. Conflict skills are hard to practice at work, because the first try is usually the real one. A simulation lets someone try an approach that would be risky with a colleague, see what happens, then go back and try another.

  • Making every choice teach something, with no single "right" path
  • Showing that each conflict strategy has a cost, and that the best one depends on the situation
  • Letting learners compare approaches without starting the whole scenario again

What I did

  • Three scenarios that rise in difficulty: an email misunderstanding (beginner, 5-8 minutes), two projects competing for one designer (intermediate, 8-12 minutes) and a team member whose performance is slipping (advanced, 10-15 minutes)
  • Feedback after every choice built on the Thomas-Kilmann model: the strategy used (collaborating, compromising, competing, accommodating or avoiding), a rating of how well it worked, a short explanation, and the skills it shows
  • Every scenario branches to several different endings: 14 in the beginner scenario, 14 in the intermediate and 11 in the advanced
  • Each scene shows the people involved, with their names and roles
  • Each ending names the outcome (positive, mixed or difficult) and asks a reflection question, and learners can go back and try another choice
  • A "Go Back" button, so learners can return to a decision and try a different strategy
  • Progress saves in the browser, so a scenario can be finished later
  • Each scenario is a separate data file, so a new one can be written without changing the code

What happened

  • Live on GitHub Pages, with all three scenarios playable from start to finish
  • Includes screen-reader announcements for story changes, and turns animations off for people who ask their device for reduced motion

Inside the course

  • Each scene sets up the situation, shows who is involved, and offers several ways to respond.

Alex sent Jordan a brief email..." and the first response choices.

  • After each choice: the strategy used, how well it worked, and why.

    Feedback card labelled COMPROMISING, rated three of five stars, Medium: "Email intervention shows good intentions but doesn't address the underlying communication style differences." Skills demonstrated: diplomatic communication, conflict de-escalation.

  • Every ending names the outcome and asks a reflection question.

    Ending card: Ending reached, Positive outcome. "Communication Bridge Built: Alex and Jordan now have a better understanding of each other's communication styles, and the team is more cohesive." A Reflect box asks "How might you apply collaborative problem-solving to other workplace communication challenges?" Buttons: Go back and try another choice, Choose another scenario.

  • Three scenarios, from beginner to advanced.

    Scenario menu: The Email Misunderstanding (beginner, 5-8 minutes), The Resource Competition (intermediate, 8-12 minutes) and The Performance Gap (advanced, 10-15 minutes).

About this Project

A web-based simulator built around workplace scenarios at beginner, intermediate and advanced levels. After each choice, it names the Thomas-Kilmann strategy the learner used, rates how well it worked, and explains why.
