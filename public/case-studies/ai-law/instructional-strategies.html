Objective Content Development Instructional Strategy
3.A

Identify different types of chatbots and their basic components as used in legal settings

This module includes 3 instructional strategy levels from the ICAP framework, these strategies are Active, Constructive, and Interactive.

• Active Learning: This strategy will be used for objectives 3.A.1 and 3.A.2, allowing learners to explore and experiment with model parameters and performance characteristics. Learners will watch instructional videos and complete hands-on exercises to understand parameter settings and their effects on model behavior.

• Constructive Learning: This strategy will be used for objectives 3.B.1 and 3.B.2. Learners will develop their understanding of AI model creation and chatbot structure through documentation exercises and RAG implementation planning. They will create their own documentation and process maps for chatbot response structures.

• Interactive Learning: This strategy will be used for objectives 3.A.3 and 3.B.3. Learners will engage in collaborative design activities for chatbot specifications and deployment strategies. This approach allows for hands-on learning while working within technical resource constraints.

Media Used

There are 3 types of media used in this module, they are:

• Instructional Video: The instructional videos will provide comprehensive demonstrations of model parameters, chatbot structure, and implementation considerations. This will give learners a visual understanding of key concepts and practical applications. (3.A.1, 3.A.2)

• Text: Text will be used throughout the module to provide reference materials, documentation templates, and guided exercises. This will serve as a foundation for understanding theoretical concepts and implementation strategies. (3.B.1, 3.B.2)

• Interactive Media: The interactive exercises and collaborative activities will allow learners to experiment with parameter settings and develop chatbot specifications in a hands-on environment. This will provide practical experience while working within technical limitations. (3.A.3, 3.B.2, 3.B.3)
3.A.1

After completing this module, learners will be able to identify different types of chatbots and their basic components as used in legal settings

○ Categorized explanations of chatbot types (client intake, legal research assistants, client service, document assembly) with legal-specific examples for each
3.A.2

After completing this module, learners will be able to explain how chatbots process user inquiries and deliver information in legal environments

Taught through step-by-step explanation of the information processing cycle, from input analysis to response generation, with legal query examples
3.A.3

After completing this module, learners will be able to evaluate which chatbot solutions address specific legal practice needs

Taught through an assessment framework and matching different legal specialties (estate planning, immigration, corporate law) to appropriate chatbot applications
3B

Learners will be able to assess no-code chatbot platforms for legal applications
3.B.1

After completing this module, learners will be able to compare leading no-code chatbot platforms based on features relevant to legal practice

Detailed platform comparison matrix analyzing legal relevance, features, price points, and best legal use cases for multiple platforms
3.B.2

After completing this module, learners will be able to select appropriate chatbot platforms that align with specific legal service objectives

Purpose-matching framework connecting different legal service goals (client acquisition, service improvement, practice efficiency) to specific platform capabilities
3.B.3

After completing this module, learners will be able to interpret pricing models and evaluate cost-benefit considerations for chatbot implementation

Explanation of pricing structures, ROI calculation formula with legal example, and identification of hidden costs specific to legal implementation
3.C

Learners will be able to analyze legal and ethical implications of chatbot use
3.C.1

After completing this module, learners will be able to identify potential data privacy and security risks associated with legal chatbots

Analysis of unique sensitivities of legal information and categorization of specific privacy/security risks in legal contexts with privacy-by-design solutions
3.C.2

After completing this module, learners will be able to apply relevant regulations and ethical guidelines to chatbot implementation in legal settings

Multi-layered compliance framework covering legal professional regulations, privacy laws, and AI-specific rules with jurisdiction considerations
3.C.3

After completing this module, learners will be able to develop strategies to ensure chatbot compliance with legal professional responsibilities

Risk assessment framework, technical and procedural safeguards, and governance structure guidelines specific to legal practice obligations

Strategy in Timeline

Active will be implemented for 6.A.1,2 and 6.B.1,2 allowing learners to consume learning material in a more active format rather than just listening to traditional instructional videos.

Constructive strategy will be implemented in 6.A.3,4 and 6.B.3,4 by asking learners to create their own examples based on given scenarios. Learners will create their own prompts in a low-stakes environment to ensure mastery of the materials.

Changes in Strategy

No, these strategies allow learners to master the material and demonstrate mastery through authentic assessments

How does this strategy support learning objectives

This strategy will introduce learners to a complex skill and break it down into a reusable process. Learners will be assessed on their ability to complete the skill, so it is an authentic assessment paired with real world learning.

Intended Outcomes of Strategy

Learners will develop a procedural knowledge in the real world and complete the learning with an understanding of how to implement these prompts in their professional lives.

How does the strategy support the content presentation and delivery

By presenting the material first in a traditional way with interactions built in so learners are more engaged, then moving to an authentic assessment type based on real world scenarios.
